The metaphor
Beneath every forest runs a network of fungal threads — mycelium — that connects the trees, carries nutrients between them, and signals when one is in distress. The forest looks like individuals. It behaves like one organism.

Your company is the same. The intelligence is already there. It's just buried, fragmented, and dying every time someone leaves. We built Mycelium to surface it.
The problem

At 50 people, the
company forgets itself.

By the time you hit fifty employees, your company runs on knowledge spread across forty different tools and dozens of heads. Wikis go stale in weeks. Onboarding is a ritual of asking the same four people the same five questions. And when someone leaves, something important leaves with them — and nobody knows what.

The product

Four things no
other tool does together.

i.

Observe
everything.

Mycelium connects to Slack, Notion, Google Workspace, Linear, GitHub, Salesforce, your meeting transcripts and email. It reads what's actually happening, not what someone remembered to document.

ii.

Map how things really work.

From observed behavior, Mycelium builds a living model of your organization: real workflows, real decision patterns, real expertise networks — not the version on the org chart.

iii.

Write its own documentation.

No more wiki rot. Mycelium drafts and continuously updates documentation from what it observes — onboarding guides, runbooks, process docs, all alive and dated to the day they were last verified.

iv.

Answer anything.

Any employee can ask "who actually owns our Stripe integration?" or "how did the refund policy evolve?" and get a current answer with sources — pulled straight from the conversations and documents where the knowledge lives.

Why now

Three forces just
made this inevitable.

i. 2024 –

Long-context AI

Million-token context windows make it possible for the first time to reason across an entire company's accumulated communications — not just retrieve from them.

ii. 2020 –

The remote shift

Hybrid work permanently fragmented the hallway conversations and shoulder-tapping that used to hold organizations together. The institutional knowledge crisis is now structural.

iii. ongoing

SaaS sprawl

The average mid-size company runs more than 80 SaaS tools — up from ~20 a decade ago. Knowledge has never been more fragmented, or more in need of a unifying layer.
